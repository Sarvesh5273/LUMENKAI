## Key Facts
- **Deadline:** Sep. 30, 2026, 11:45 p.m. PDT. Ten calendar dates remain, but actual time depends on the current hour. Official rules: https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules
- **India:** India is not named among excluded jurisdictions. This is not a guarantee that every Indian entrant is lawful/eligible; local law and prize-receipt restrictions still apply. General entrants must have reached their local age of majority. Same rules URL.
- **Next Gen exception:** An active student aged 13+ may enter Next Gen; a minor needs guardian consent. Proof is a qualifying student/academic email on Devpost, potentially checked via JetBrains/swot. A BTech enrollment alone is insufficient if the email cannot be verified. https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules and https://www.shipaton.com/categories/next-gen-award
- **Teams:** Official rules describe teams as teams of eligible individuals. Safest reading: every member must independently qualify; a team containing a minor can compete only in Next Gen. The evidence does not clearly establish whether every member must independently supply academic-email proof, so confirm with organizers before relying on a mixed team. Same URLs.
- **No store launch:** General awards require a first public release during Aug. 1–Sep. 30 on Apple App Store, Google Play, or Samsung Galaxy Store. Next Gen substitutes a demo video plus a public open-source repository and license; no paid developer account/store release is required. Therefore, an unlaunched project appears eligible **only for Next Gen**, assuming all Next Gen conditions are met. Rules URL.
- **RevenueCat:** Official rules require RevenueCat SDK powering at least one in-app/web purchase **or** RevenueCat Ads. No Next Gen exemption was identified in the rules; the Next Gen marketing page’s omission should not be treated as an exemption. Rules URL.
- **Timing/reuse:** Work may have begun before Aug. 1; it simply cannot have had an earlier eligible-store public release. Updates to previously released apps are ineligible. Open-source dependencies are allowed subject to licenses and meaningful enhancement. https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/announcing-shipaton-2026
- **AI/existing-code reuse:** No specific AI-code permission/prohibition or comprehensive rule for proprietary reused code was located. Do not infer permission beyond the explicit pre-existing-unreleased-project and licensed open-source allowances.
- **Judging:** Criteria vary by category; most have no numerical weights. The official-rules extraction found only isolated weighting (e.g. Galaxy optimization 20%), not a Next Gen numerical rubric. Homepage names Charlie Chapman and David Barnard, while rules reserve judge selection to Sponsor and say judges may or may not be listed. https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/ and rules URL.
- **Stacking:** FAQ says Next Gen plus another category is possible if both code and store submission requirements are met; the store listing is ignored for Next Gen judging. Influencer awards are mutually exclusive with each other but may stack with eligible non-Influencer categories. https://www.shipaton.com/faq
- **Prize conflict:** Official-rules evidence says Next Gen $15k/$10k/$5k, while homepage and Next Gen page say $20k/$10k/$5k. This unresolved first-prize discrepancy materially weakens expected-value calculations; obtain written confirmation. 

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- “A BTech student can enter” depends on academic-email verification, age/consent, team composition, and RevenueCat implementation—not merely student status.
- “No launch needed” applies to Next Gen, not the rest of the competition.
- Marketing pages are inconsistent with the fetched official rules on first prize and less complete on RevenueCat requirements.
- **Skeptical recommendation:** Enter only if a credible demo, public licensed repo, qualifying academic email, and RevenueCat integration can be completed without harming coursework. With ten days and an unresolved prize amount, do not justify a major time/cash spend by the advertised top prize alone.
